I design large-scale MILP optimization methods for industrial planning problems that are too big for off-the-shelf solvers. Through a three-year MITACS-funded collaboration with Rio Tinto's mine-planning team, my cutting-plane and rolling-horizon methods cut mine-scheduling computation time by **90%** and time-to-feasibility by **80%** — deployed directly into Rio Tinto's production planning workflows, not left on the shelf as a research prototype.

My doctoral work at Polytechnique Montréal and GERAD addresses the Open-Pit Mine Production Scheduling Problem, combining mixed-integer programming, cutting planes, relax-and-repair heuristics, and — currently — graph neural networks and machine learning that predict which MILP instances will be hard to solve and guide the solver accordingly.

I'm looking at Optimization Scientist, ML Optimization Engineer, and Operations Research Scientist roles on an industrial Data & AI team, particularly in mining, transportation, and other large-scale industrial planning settings, following my Ph.D. defense (targeted April–May 2027).
